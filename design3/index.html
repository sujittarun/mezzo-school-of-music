The musical three

You asked how usable the Score actually is. Honestly: it was the prettiest of the five and the weakest of the five, for three reasons — eighty children meant twenty near-identical staves, the vertical axis carried the instrument but you mark by reading the fill, so the height was decoration during the only task that matters, and finding one named child was hard.

These three keep the music and fix that. They are not three skins; they are the three screens of one app, each borrowing a different real object from music — a conductor's score, a tuner, a player-piano roll — and each borrowing it because the object already solves the problem, not because it looks like music.

All three are live — tap them in the previews. Notes mark and sweep, the tuner glides into pitch and rings, the roll punches. They run on twenty-two children rather than eight, because eight never showed the crowding.
What to tell me: which of the three feels like the register he would open every afternoon. They can be mixed — the Roll could be the month behind the Score's today, and the Tuner could be the dues tab under either.
